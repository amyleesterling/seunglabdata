About the game

What is Pyr?

Pyr (pronounced "peer") is the Seung Lab's brain mapping game at Princeton University. You proofread real neurons in pictures of the brain, and every fix helps build a connectome: a complete wiring diagram of a brain.

Is Pyr the same as EyeWire II?

Yes. Pyr was called EyeWire II while we built it. Today EyeWire II is the name of its first dataset, the mouse retina. Pyr follows the original EyeWire, which the Seung Lab launched in 2012.

What do players map?

Pyr covers several brains, not just one. Players map neurons in the mouse retina (EyeWire II) and trace axons in the medial entorhinal cortex (MEC). They practice in a Sandbox, and can explore other connectomes such as hippocampus CA3, FlyWire, BANC and MICrONS. The CA3 project at pyr.ai is one part of Pyr.

How do I join?

Pyr is in a closed beta, so it's invite only for now. Request early access and we'll email you when you're in. No science background needed.

Where is the latest news?

Right here on connectome.quest: the blog for news and guides, Help for how to play, and the forum for questions. Older posts about EyeWire II on other forums may be out of date.

Who makes Pyr?

Pyr is made by the Seung Lab at the Princeton Neuroscience Institute, Princeton University. It was designed and built by Amy Sterling. See the credits for everyone involved.

Why does it matter?

Computers trace neurons well but still make mistakes. People are very good at spotting them. Fixes made by players become part of real research datasets that scientists around the world use to learn how brains work.
