About the game

What is Pyr?

Pronounced "peer". Formerly EyeWire II.

Pyr is a brain mapping game where you proofread real neurons in pictures of the brain. Every fix you make helps build a connectome: a complete wiring diagram of a brain.

Created by Amy Sterling at the Seung Lab, Princeton University

Request early access Latest news

01

Is Pyr the same as EyeWire II?

Yes. Pyr was called EyeWire II while we built it. Today EyeWire II is the name of its first dataset, the mouse retina. Pyr follows the original EyeWire, which the Seung Lab launched in 2012.

02

What do players map?

Pyr covers several brains, not just one. Players map neurons in the mouse retina (EyeWire II) and trace axons in the medial entorhinal cortex (MEC). They practice in a Sandbox, and can explore other connectomes such as hippocampus CA3, FlyWire, BANC and MICrONS. The CA3 project at pyr.ai is one part of Pyr.

03

How do I join?

Pyr is in a closed beta, so it's invite only for now. Request early access and we'll email you when you're in. No science background needed.

04

Where is the latest news?

05

Why does it matter?

Computers trace neurons well but still make mistakes, and people are very good at spotting them. Fixes made by players become part of real research datasets that scientists around the world use to learn how brains work.

06

Who made Pyr?

Amy Sterling (@amyneurons) created Pyr, designing and building the game at the Seung Lab, Princeton Neuroscience Institute. See the credits for everyone who helped.
