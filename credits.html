About this project

Credits

Connectome Quest and Pyr bring together public datasets, learning tools, and ways for people to contribute to connectomics. Here is who makes it possible.

Pyr

Pyr, the game where players proofread real connectomes, was designed and built by Amy Sterling, @amyneurons, at Seung Lab. It grew out of EyeWire and runs on Neuroglancer and CAVE.

With thanks to Celia David, Marissa Sorek, Ryan Morey, Will Silversmith, Chris Jordan, Jay Gager, Eric Hammersmith, Zhihao Zheng, Arie Matsliah and Nikitas Seratafinidis.

Research community

Connectome Quest is presented by Seung Lab at the Princeton Neuroscience Institute, Princeton University. The fly datasets are made in collaboration with Murthy Lab, also at Princeton.

Support

Google and Amazon Web Services provide Seung Lab with the cloud compute that stores these datasets and serves them to every player. The research is funded by the National Institutes of Health (NIH), including the BRAIN Initiative and BRAIN CONNECTS, and by the Princeton Neuroscience Institute, with support for individual datasets from IARPA, the Simons Foundation, Research to Prevent Blindness and others listed with each dataset below. Thank you.

Educational experiences

Neuro 101 introduces the brain across scales. Connectome 101, the interactive Connectome Atlas, was created by Amy Sterling, @amyneurons.

Seung Lab datasets

The connectomes you can explore and proofread here, the teams behind them, and where to read about them.

EyeWire II: Mouse Retina

Seung Lab, Princeton University, with Euler Lab and Berens Lab, University of Tübingen, the Eyewire II Consortium and the players of Pyr.

Pyr's Level 1 dataset: a 1 × 1 × 0.09 mm volume of adult mouse retina with about 100,000 neurons, spanning every synaptic layer. eyewire.ai

  • Ströh, S. et al. Eyewire II – A connectomic resource for resolving cell types and circuits of the mouse retina. bioRxiv (2026). doi:10.64898/2026.05.28.727403

Funding: Princeton Neuroscience Institute and the NIH (5U01N5090562, Seung and Euler labs); DFG (SPP 2041, 313856816, Euler and Berens labs); ERC (NextMechMod, 101039115) and the Hertie Foundation (Berens lab); the Research to Prevent Blindness Transformational Team Science Award. Compute from Amazon and Google.

Medial Entorhinal Cortex (MEC)

Seung Lab and the Princeton Neuroscience Institute, with the players of Pyr.

Grid cell circuitry in mouse medial entorhinal cortex, where players trace axons and Super Quest interneurons. More about MEC.

Publication in preparation.

FlyWire: the whole adult fly brain

FlyWire Consortium, led by Seung Lab and Murthy Lab, Princeton. Images from the FAFB volume.

The first complete wiring diagram of an adult brain, 139,255 neurons, proofread by a worldwide community. flywire.ai · Codex

  • Dorkenwald, S. et al. Neuronal wiring diagram of an adult brain. Nature 634, 124–138 (2024). doi:10.1038/s41586-024-07558-y
  • Schlegel, P. et al. Whole-brain annotation and multi-connectome cell typing of Drosophila. Nature 634, 139–152 (2024). doi:10.1038/s41586-024-07686-5
  • Lin, A., Yang, R. et al. Network statistics of the whole-brain connectome of Drosophila. Nature 634, 153–165 (2024). doi:10.1038/s41586-024-07968-y
  • Dorkenwald, S. et al. FlyWire: online community for whole-brain connectomics. Nature Methods 19, 119–128 (2022). doi:10.1038/s41592-021-01330-0
  • Zheng, Z. et al. A complete electron microscopy volume of the brain of adult Drosophila melanogaster. Cell 174, 730–743 (2018). doi:10.1016/j.cell.2018.06.019

Funding: NIH BRAIN Initiative (RF1 MH117815, RF1 MH129268, U24 NS126935, RF1 MH120679), Wellcome Trust (203261/Z/16/Z, 220343/Z/20/Z), Medical Research Council (MC-U105188491, MC_EX_MR/T046279/1), NSF Neuronex2 (2014862), Princeton Neuroscience Institute and IARPA. Compute from Amazon and Google.

BANC: Fly Brain and Nerve Cord

Seung Lab and Murthy Lab, Princeton, with Wilson Lab and Lee Lab at Harvard Medical School and many collaborators.

The first connectome of a fly's whole central nervous system, brain and ventral nerve cord together. Explore in Codex

  • Bates, A. S. et al. Distributed control circuits across a brain-and-cord connectome. Nature (2026). doi:10.1038/s41586-026-10735-w

MICrONS: Mouse Visual Cortex (Pinky and Minnie)

MICrONS Consortium: the Allen Institute for Brain Science, Baylor College of Medicine and Seung Lab, Princeton.

Functional connectomics of mouse visual cortex: a cubic millimetre whose neurons' activity was recorded before imaging. Pyr's Sandbox uses the smaller Pinky volume. MICrONS Explorer

  • MICrONS Consortium. Functional connectomics spanning multiple areas of mouse visual cortex. Nature 640, 435–447 (2025). doi:10.1038/s41586-025-08790-w
  • Turner, N. L. et al. Reconstruction of neocortex: organelles, compartments, cells, circuits, and activity. Cell 185, 1082–1100 (2022). doi:10.1016/j.cell.2022.01.023

Funding: IARPA MICrONS project (D16PC0005), for the Seung lab and the Allen Institute for Brain Science. Compute from Amazon and Google.

CA3: Mouse Hippocampus

Seung Lab and Tank Lab, Princeton University.

Pyramidal cells of hippocampal area CA3 and the more than 55,000 mossy fiber axons that feed them. On the cover of Nature Neuroscience. Explore CA3

  • Zheng, Z. et al. Hippocampal CA3 connectomics reveals a gradient of mossy fiber inputs and selective feedforward inhibition onto pyramidal cells. Nature Neuroscience 29, 2311–2323 (2026). doi:10.1038/s41593-026-02388-9

Funding: NIH BRAIN Initiative (U19 NS104648, U19 NS132720, RF1 MH123400) and BRAIN CONNECTS (UM1 NS132250), an NIH instrumentation award (S10 OD023602), the Simons Foundation, Princeton Neuroscience Institute and Google; Zhihao Zheng by a Pathway to Independence Award (K99 NS135650). Compute from Amazon and Google. Part of the NIH BRAIN CONNECTS project to map the whole mouse hippocampal formation. pyr.ai

EyeWire: the original retina

Seung Lab, then at MIT, with the EyeWire player community. Retina volume e2198 imaged by Kevin Briggman, Moritz Helmstaedter and Winfried Denk at the Max Planck Institute.

Where citizen neuroscience began: players reconstructed retinal neurons in 3D. eyewire.org

  • Kim, J. S. et al. Space–time wiring specificity supports direction selectivity in the retina. Nature 509, 331–336 (2014). doi:10.1038/nature13240
  • Bae, J. A. et al. Digital museum of retinal ganglion cells with dense anatomy and physiology. Cell 173, 1293–1306 (2018). doi:10.1016/j.cell.2018.04.040
  • Briggman, K. L., Helmstaedter, M. & Denk, W. Wiring specificity in the direction-selectivity circuit of the retina. Nature 471, 183–188 (2011). doi:10.1038/nature09818
