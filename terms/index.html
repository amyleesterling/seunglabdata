Pyr in plain words

This short version is here to help. The full terms below are what count.

  • Pyr is a science game run by the Seung Lab at Princeton University. You help map real neurons.
  • Your merges, cuts and cell notes go into shared research datasets, with your username attached as the author. They can't be taken back out, because other people's work builds on them.
  • Your username, profile, achievements and chat messages are public. Your email address is never shown to other players.
  • We record how you play (edits, tutorial steps, time in the game) to improve Pyr and to study how people and computers map the brain together.
  • Questions you ask the AI Guide are answered by Claude, an AI made by Anthropic, and saved so we can improve the answers.
  • We never sell your data and never show ads.
  • We send a few emails about your account and Pyr news. One click stops them.
  • You must be 13 or older. If you are under 18, a parent or guardian needs to agree too.
  • Want your account deleted? Email support@eyewire.ai and we will do it. Your edits stay in the science, but without your name on them.

2. Privacy Notice

Pyr respects and protects the privacy of your personal data. This notice explains what Pyr collects, why, who else handles it, and what you can do about it.

What we collect

  • Account: your email address and CAVE user ID (from signing in), your name, Pyr username, and anything you add to your profile (bio, avatar, country flag). We also keep your time zone so daily streaks and awards match your day. Your email and time zone are never shown to other players.
  • If you ask to join: what you tell us in the request form (for example your name, how you found Pyr, your EyeWire name, and a note), and notes staff write while reviewing it.
  • Gameplay: your edits and annotations, the cells you finish, tutorial progress (including how long each step took), proofreading test results, achievements, help you ask for and give, and when you were last active.
  • Chat: global chat and team chat messages you post. Global chat is public and is stored. We look at chat to keep the community safe and may study it in aggregate for research. Because chat is public, we cannot stop others from copying it.
  • Bug reports and help requests: what you write, the page you were on, and, if included, a screenshot and your browser's console log. Screenshots are deleted automatically after 14 days unless still needed for an open report.
  • AI Guide: the questions you ask the Guide, its answers, and what was on your screen in Pyr (such as the dataset and open panels), along with your username.
  • Technical: your IP address, browser and device type, and error reports when something breaks in Pyr. We use these to keep Pyr running, fix bugs and prevent abuse.
  • Saved settings: Pyr stores your sign in and your game preferences in your own browser (local storage). Pyr does not use advertising or tracking cookies, and does not use third party analytics.

Pyr will not ask for personal data revealing racial or ethnic origin, political opinions, religious or philosophical beliefs, genetic information, health information, or information about sex life or orientation.

Why we use it

  • To run Pyr: your account, progress, achievements, leaderboards and chat.
  • To credit you for your work, in Pyr and in publications.
  • To help you: answering bug reports, help requests and emails.
  • To improve Pyr and its tutorials, and to keep the community safe.
  • For research into how people and computers map the brain together. Data and analysis may appear in the game, on our websites, and in publications, presentations and other public venues. We will ask your permission before publishing analysis that is tied to your username. Your username may still appear in screenshots, videos and demonstrations of the game.

Credit and real names

You can choose to give your real name for credit on scientific papers. Without it, we use your username. Contributors' names or usernames may be listed with the cells they helped reconstruct when those cells are released outside Pyr. If you are a professional scientist using Pyr for your lab's work, we may ask for your real name, email, institution, city, lab head and research interests, and share these with other Pyr users for collaboration.

Who else handles your data

We never sell your data and never share it for advertising. We use these service providers to run Pyr, and each handles only what it needs:

  • CAVE and the connectomics data servers (run by Seung Lab and research partners): sign in, and storing your edits and annotations in the research datasets.
  • Supabase: the main game database, chat and file storage.
  • Google Cloud and Firebase: the servers that run Pyr, and staff spreadsheets used to manage beta access (Google Sheets).
  • Resend: sending Pyr emails.
  • Anthropic: the AI Guide, and AI tools that help staff sort bug reports.
  • Slack and GitHub: the team's workspace, where bug reports and flagged Guide answers are discussed and fixed.
  • Google Fonts: loads the fonts Pyr uses, which shares your IP address with Google.

We may also share data with consultants or advisors who help run or secure the Site, and with government or law enforcement when the law requires it, or to protect the rights and safety of players and others.

Emails

We email you from Pyr about your account (for example, when you are let in), a small number of reminders if you have not started, and Pyr news such as new datasets. Every email that is not a direct reply to you has a one click unsubscribe link. Unsubscribing stops these emails; it does not affect your account. Staff and other players may also reach you through chat in the game.

Sending data to other countries

Pyr is run from the United States, and its service providers store data in the United States and possibly other countries. If you use Pyr from outside the United States, your data will be transferred to the United States, which may not have the same data protection laws as your home country. Contact support@eyewire.ai for information about the safeguards in place.

How long we keep it

  • Your contributions to datasets are kept permanently, because the science depends on them.
  • Account and gameplay data are kept while your account exists, and for as long as needed to credit you for your work.
  • Screenshots in bug reports are deleted after 14 days unless still needed.
  • We may keep data that no longer identifies you indefinitely for research and statistics, and keep other data where the law requires it.

Children

Pyr is not for children under 13. If we learn that a player is under 13, we will delete their account and personal data unless we can obtain verified consent from a parent or guardian. Parents may contact support@eyewire.ai at any time to review or delete their child's data.

Your choices and rights

  • Delete your account: email support@eyewire.ai from your sign in address. We will delete your personal data from Pyr and the services above, and stop all emails. Your contributions to the datasets stay, but we remove your username from them and they become anonymous. Your name stays on any papers already published. Deleted accounts cannot be restored.
  • See or correct your data: email us for a copy of the personal data we hold about you, or to fix anything wrong.
  • Stop emails: use the unsubscribe link in any email.
  • Object or ask questions: email support@eyewire.ai.

For players in the European Union and United Kingdom

The EU General Data Protection Regulation ("GDPR") may apply to personal data you provide while you are in the EU or UK. Pyr relies on these legal bases:

  • Legitimate interests: providing a platform where members of the public reconstruct neural circuits in shared datasets and contribute to science; promoting Pyr, including sharing scientific results and recruiting new players; running the Site efficiently; and making sure Princeton University's legal obligations are met.
  • Legal obligation: for example, disclosure to courts or authorities where legally required.
  • Vital interests: to protect someone from serious harm.
  • Public interest: providing a collaborative scientific platform for the public.

Subject to certain limits, you have the right to access, correct, erase (except contributions to the datasets), restrict, move, and object to the processing of your personal data, including objecting to emails. If we refuse a request, we will tell you why. You can also complain to your local data protection authority.
